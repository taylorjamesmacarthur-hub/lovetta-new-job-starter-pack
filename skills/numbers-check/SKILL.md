# Numbers check

## Starting bars

| Signal | Bar | If below, it's usually... |
|---|---|---|
| Conversations per 100 dials | 8–10 | **Data or timing.** Check direct lines, spam flags on your number, and call before 9am or after 4:30pm. |
| Conversation → meeting | 3 in 10 | **The opener or the reason.** Lead with a colleague's name or a client story. |
| Held ÷ booked | 90%+ | **Confirmation.** You asked for time instead of giving a reason to show. Send the agenda the day before. |
| Held → next step | 1 in 2 | **Qualification.** Run `ae-handoff-note` step 1 before booking. |
| Email replies (supports the call) | 5%+ | **The message.** Rewrite it around the broker's client problem, not the product. |

These are starting bars, not gospel. After 30 days, swap in the team's real numbers.

## What to return

1. **The numbers as rates**, next to the bars.
2. **The single leakiest stage.** Fix the top of the funnel first: there's no point fixing show rate if nobody picks up.
3. **One change for tomorrow:** something concrete you can test in a single call block.
4. **One line for your AE or manager:** what you're seeing and what you're trying next.

Keep it short. A bad day is not a bad month, so if the sample is under 50 dials, say that before you draw conclusions.
